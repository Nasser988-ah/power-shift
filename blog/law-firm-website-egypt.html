What a law firm website in Egypt should show

Someone with a legal problem is comparing names. The site should say which work you take, who the lawyer is, and how to send the facts.

What a law firm website in Egypt should show

A person looking for a lawyer is rarely browsing. They have a dispute, a contract, or a filing date. They open three sites and keep the one that tells them, quickly, whether this office does that kind of work in their city.

Name the work, the lawyer, and the city

“Full legal services” is not a practice. Write the areas you actually take: companies, family, criminal, property, labour. Put the lawyer's name on the page, not only the office brand. Say the city, and whether meetings are in the office or remote. A stock photograph of a gavel does none of this.

Show how a person should instruct you

The next question is practical: what do I send, and who reads it? A short note is enough — call, WhatsApp, or email, and which documents help on the first contact. Do not build a long form that asks for the whole case before anyone has replied. The same rule sits in how a WhatsApp button should work.

Do not promise the outcome

  • Do not guarantee a win, a settlement, or a timeline the court does not control.
  • Do not publish client names unless you have permission.
  • Do not invent case counts.

Describe the work. Leave the result to the matter itself. A clinic site has the same limit — it must not promise a cure — and a law site must not promise a judgment.

A page we actually built

The site for Radwan Ezz El-Arab is a law office website: the practice, the lawyer, and a way to make contact. It is not a directory of every legal topic in Egypt. That narrower job is what most offices need.

If you are still deciding what a company site must contain before the practice pages, start with what a business website needs. When the brief is clear, a custom website is the build — not a template with a courthouse photo.
