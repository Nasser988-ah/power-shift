When should you redesign your business website?

A redesign is expensive. Do it when the current site blocks enquiries or misrepresents the work — not because a competitor launched a darker theme.

Nasser Ahmed Published 31 August 2026 Updated 31 August 2026

Websites

When should you redesign your business website?

Owners feel embarrassed by an old site before they feel the operational pain. Embarrassment is not a brief. The brief is: what can this URL not do that the business now needs.

Signals that justify a rebuild

  • Nobody on the team can change a price or a photo without calling the original developer — who is gone.
  • The site is English-only and the buyers are not.
  • It is slow or broken on phones. See why that costs you the visit.
  • You now sell a shop or a system and the site is still a 2018 brochure.
  • Google shows an old host, or the pages are not in the index. Start with indexing before you restyle.

Signals that do not

A trend in rounded corners. A founder who saw a Dribbble shot. A request to “make it more premium” with no change in offer, proof, or contact path. You can waste a full project budget and keep the same empty inbox.

Refresh versus rebuild

If the URLs, languages, and conversion path are right, you may only need clearer writing and lighter images. If the structure is a single scrolling poster, start from the pages a company site needs. Cost still follows that scope: what changes the price.

Do not throw away what ranks

If a service URL already brings the right enquiries, keep the path. Redesign the template around it. A full domain change for taste is how you reset search to zero.

When the site must match a live product again, that is website work — sometimes with examples of shipped products as the proof, not a moodboard.
