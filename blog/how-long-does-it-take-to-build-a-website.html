How long does it take to build a website?

The build is rarely the slow part. Waiting for copy, photos and decisions is. Here is what actually moves the date, and what is in your hands.

Nasser Ahmed Published 28 September 2026 Updated 28 September 2026

Websites

How long does it take to build a website?

Owners usually ask this right after the price question, and for the same reason: they have a launch, a campaign, or a season in mind. The honest answer is that there is no fixed number of days for “a website”. There is a timeline for your website once the scope is written.

POWER SHIFT puts the phases and dates in the scope before work starts. This note explains what moves those dates, so you can recognise a realistic plan from a promise made to win the job.

The five things that decide the timeline

  1. Size: a few company pages is a different job from a catalogue with hundreds of products.
  2. Content: whether copy and photos exist, or someone still has to write and shoot them.
  3. Languages: Arabic and English with proper RTL is two versions of every page, not a switch.
  4. Decisions: how many people must approve, and how fast they answer.
  5. Integrations: payment, shipping, booking or anything that talks to another system.

Content is the usual delay

Most stalled projects are not waiting on code. They are waiting for an “About” paragraph, a price list, or product photos. If content is ready on day one, the build moves at the speed of design and review. If it is not, the site sits finished around empty boxes.

The fix is boring and effective: agree who writes what, and by when, inside the scope. If you want the studio to write copy, say so early — it is its own piece of work.

What the phases look like

  1. Brief and scope: pages, languages, what happens after a visitor clicks, and what can wait.
  2. Structure and design: the page layout and the path to a call, WhatsApp message or order.
  3. Build: a preview link you open on your own phone.
  4. Review: the agreed rounds of changes, in writing.
  5. Launch: domain, links, forms and Google indexing checked before going live.

Each phase waits on a decision from you before the next starts. That is where most calendar time goes.

How to keep your project short

  • Name one person who can approve.
  • Send content before design starts, even if it is rough.
  • Launch version one with what matters, and list the rest for version two.
  • Decide early whether you need a shop or a catalogue — they are different jobs.
  • Sort the domain and hosting access in week one — see what you should own.

Be careful with “ready in 48 hours”

A template can be online quickly. A site with your real services, your Arabic, and a working enquiry path cannot skip the content and review steps. If a quote promises a date before anyone has asked what pages you need, it is a package, not a plan. Choosing a website company is partly about hearing that difference.
