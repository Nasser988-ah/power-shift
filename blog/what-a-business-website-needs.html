What a business website actually needs

Most business sites fail because they are decorated, not because they are missing features. Here is the short list that has to be true.

Nasser Ahmed Published 31 August 2026 Updated 31 August 2026

Websites

What a business website actually needs

A business website has one job: let a stranger decide whether to contact you. Everything else is optional. Sliders, stock photos of handshakes, and a blog you will never update do not do that job.

Pages that earn their keep

  • Home: who you are, who you serve, what happens next — in the first screen on a phone.
  • What you sell: services or products as separate pages if people search for them, not one paragraph dumping everything.
  • Proof: live work, not adjectives. If you cannot show work, say what you do in concrete sentences.
  • How to start: one path. In Egypt that is often WhatsApp plus a form, not five competing buttons.

If you only need a campaign for one offer, you may need a landing page instead of a full site. If you sell many things to returning customers, you need the site.

Trust without fake claims

Do not invent client logos, visitor counts, or awards. A clear address city, a real phone number, and work you can open in a browser beat a “500+ happy clients” line that nobody can check. POWER SHIFT shows live products on the work page for the same reason.

Language and the phone

If your buyers read Arabic, the site must be Arabic — not an English site with a translate widget. RTL done badly looks like the company did not care. If you sell to the GCC as well, plan both languages from the start: bilingual sites are a structure decision, not a toggle.

More than half of visits will be on a phone. Tiny type, sticky popups, and click-to-call that is not the real number waste the visit. Speed is part of this: a slow site is a closed shop on 4G.

What to leave out of version one

  • A blog with no editor and no topics.
  • Member logins “for later”.
  • A shop when you still confirm every order in chat — unless you are ready for catalogue versus checkout.
  • Animations that delay the first useful sentence.

After they arrive

The page is wasted if the next step is vague. Turning visits into leads is copy and one obvious action, not a CRM you will not use. When the list above is true, you are ready to talk about building the site — not about themes.
