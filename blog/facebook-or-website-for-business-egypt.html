Do you need a website if you already have Facebook?

Most Egyptian SMEs live in Facebook and WhatsApp. A website is not automatically the next step — unless someone is already trying to check you.

Nasser Ahmed Published 31 August 2026 Updated 31 August 2026

Websites

Do you need a website if you already have Facebook?

If your orders already arrive as WhatsApp voice notes, a website can feel like an expensive brochure. Sometimes it is. The question is not “is Facebook outdated?” It is: what happens when a serious buyer looks you up and Facebook is all they find.

What Facebook is actually good at

  • Reach people who already scroll there.
  • Show daily proof: stock, offers, replies.
  • Start a chat without a form.

For a stall, a one-person trade, or a brand that only sells to people who already follow it, that can be the whole machine. Do not buy a site to feel modern.

Where Facebook stops

You do not own the URL. Pages get restricted. Search on Google often shows a messy profile instead of a clear sentence about what you sell. A procurement officer, a GCC buyer, or a hotel supplier will not send a deposit from a Facebook screenshot.

Facebook is also a weak place to explain anything longer than a caption: delivery rules, wholesale terms, destinations, certifications. That copy belongs on pages you control.

The check that decides it

Search your company name plus what you sell. If the first useful result is a Facebook page with no hours, no address, and a chat button, you are asking strangers to trust a profile. If you already get “send us your website” in email, you are late.

A site does not replace the page. It sits behind it: the link in the bio, the thing you send when WhatsApp is not enough. The site still has to turn a visit into a message — usually WhatsApp in this market.

What a first website should not try to be

It should not be a second Instagram. It needs a small set of pages that answer the buying questions, load on a phone, and lead to one action. If you also need Arabic and English for the Gulf, that is not a Facebook about section — it is a bilingual build.

A practical split

  • Stay on Facebook if every customer already knows you and never asks for a link.
  • Add a site when you quote companies, export, hire, or want to appear for the service you sell — not only your brand name.

Cost still follows the job. Read what changes website cost in Egypt before you compare Facebook ads to a build. If you want the public face done properly, that is custom website work.
